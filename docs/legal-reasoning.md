# Official rules and CPU condition proposals

[report.md](../report.md) is authoritative. `legal.py` provides local BM25/exact
lookup; `legal_reasoning.py` verifies enacted official sources, clauses, parent
context, notices, draft cards and reviewed edition applicability. Current twelve
cards remain draft; three acts do not establish exhaustive RK-law coverage.

The vision model produces chronological source/actor-bound observations and
counterevidence only. `condition_evidence` builds the same actor-specific packet
for Laya and jury projection replay. Earlier role/context, visibility, contradictory
facts and authorized metadata remain present. Unknown ownership stays unresolved.

Laya receives only that structured text and a short positive proposition with
three explicit choices. A resident CPU FP32 service uses the pinned upstream
sequence builder and tokenizer, counts instruction/options/state together,
refuses state/instruction/option truncation at 1,024 tokens and batches at most
four rows. Overflow/unavailable packets return unknown. Probabilities are retained
as proposals, never interpreted as calibrated correctness or legal approval.
No images, arbitrary embeddings, legal prose generation, dense index or reranker.

Code validates references, actor binding, source ownership, repeated role evidence,
condition polarity, prerequisites/exceptions, authorized nonvisual metadata,
complete-opportunity requirements and edition applicability. A source frame alone
does not establish its semantic relevance. Conflicts stay unknown; drafts cannot
become supported legal conclusions. Explanations and citations use deterministic
templates and exact verified provisions. Inventory coverage records unevaluated
work; retrieval without candidates cannot establish clearance.

`forte legal-search --release legal/2026-10-17 --query 'кассовая операция'`
searches only the local official corpus. No real model compatibility, legal quality,
CPU timing or private-video accuracy is established by local synthetic tests.
